On Sunday, May 10, 2026, in Milan, at the editorial offices of Scomodo, we hosted a crucial meeting to learn how to quantify the economic exploitation carried out by social media platforms on our daily behaviors[^1].

We are not just passive \"users\": we are unpaid producers of informational value[^2]. During the event, we debuted the first tools designed to estimate how much money Big Tech extracts from our daily lives.

### Mapping the Exploitation
Our ultimate goal is to coordinate thousands of people to demand a collective return of the value extracted from us. The main areas of behavior we measure to construct these estimates are:
* **Presence**: Regular active account years.
* **Time**: Daily minutes spent on platforms.
* **Activity**: Interactive engagements (likes, comments, clicks).
* **Output**: User-generated content (posts, videos, reviews).

In the coming weeks, we will expand these tools to other platforms (such as TikTok and YouTube) to refine our calculations and grow our trans-national digital claim.

[^1]: On the systematic extraction of human experience as a financial raw material, see Zuboff, S. (2019), *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism*.
[^2]: For the foundational analysis of unpaid "free labor" and the user as a value generator in the platform economy, see Terranova, T. (2000), *Free Labor: Producing Culture for the Digital Economy*.
