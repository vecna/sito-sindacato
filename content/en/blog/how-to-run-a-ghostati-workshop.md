A **Ghòstati!** workshop is a group experiment. It shows how badly a local face-recognition system can work, and how a few gestures can throw it off.

## How does it work?

Open [the lab](/ghostati/), “save” your clean face, paint it, return to the webcam and adjust the makeup until the system either loses the face or stops associating it with the original reference.

## A short shopping list

This guide is for anyone who wants to facilitate a session without knowing the code. You do not need a fully equipped lab. You need a place where people can see the screen clearly. Outdoors, a television works better than a projector. In strong light, the webcam must be able to handle overexposure.

{{< media_text src="/images/ghostati/workshops/pennelli-1-6-10.jpg" side="left" variant="checklist" alt="Four makeup brushes in different shapes and sizes" caption="Fine and broad brushes let you try lines, details and blocks of colour." >}}

### Checklist

- [ ] a computer with a webcam and access to [sindacato.nina.watch/ghostati](/ghostati/);
- [ ] a projector or a large television;
- [ ] a place without direct sunlight behind the webcam, and not too bright for the screen;
- [ ] water-based face paint in both very light and very dark colours;
- [ ] brushes in different sizes: we use **1, 6 and 10 mm** brushes;
- [ ] at least one brush for each pair of participants;
- [ ] several cups of water: with only one, dark pigments quickly muddy the lighter colours;
- [ ] paper or cloths for drying brushes and cleaning the table;
- [ ] optional: mirrors and makeup remover. Water is normally enough for water-based paint;
- [ ] the [gallery from documented Ghòstati! workshops](/en/ghostati/gallery/), to inspire new patterns.

{{< /media_text >}}

### Of course, this guide is no substitute for cosmetic advice

We use products sold as “face paint suitable for children”, expecting them to have been tested. But we are not cosmetics experts, and we do not know each person’s allergies or sensitivities. Always ask before applying a product.

## How long does it take?

There is no reliable “minutes per participant” formula: some people quickly find an effective intervention, while others need several rounds. It also depends on whether participants paint one another or a small number of selected people paint everyone else.

<dl class="ghostati-timebox">
  <dt>20 minutes before</dt><dd>Set up the paint, water, table, webcam and projection.</dd>
  <dt>10–20 minutes</dt><dd>Explain <em>dazzling</em>, show examples and leave room for questions.</dd>
  <dt>About 30 minutes</dt><dd>Save the clean faces, apply makeup, and alternate adjustments with checks. This is a practical allowance, not a guarantee.</dd>
  <dt>Optional</dt><dd>A closing discussion. People often wash, stay to watch or leave at different times, so do not treat it as compulsory.</dd>
</dl>

With a large group, the bottleneck is saving everyone’s clean face on the computer. Painting can happen in parallel, provided there are enough brushes, water cups and working pairs.

## 1. Set up the space

Place the computer so that one person can stand in front of the webcam without blocking everyone else’s view. Project the lab page onto a wall or mirror it on a large television. One computer is enough. Anyone with another device can open the same address and work in parallel; the gallery includes a QR code ready to scan.

An indoor room is the simplest option. If sunlight enters behind the webcam, the face becomes dark; if the room is too bright, the projection becomes unreadable. Before opening, test the place where people will stand. They can check the result in one corner and apply makeup somewhere else.

## 2. Explain dazzling with images, not a lecture

The point is not to “hide” a face from human eyes. *Dazzling* uses contrast, interruptions and asymmetry to change the features an automated system extracts from an image.

Show a quick selection from the [gallery](/en/ghostati/gallery/). Always distinguish between:

- a **preparation** or **work-in-progress** photograph;
- a **visual result** for which we do not necessarily have a measurement;
- a **documented check**, where we know whether the face was lost or the identity crossed the threshold.

**Remember:** every pattern is individual. What works for one person will not necessarily work for someone else.

## 3. Save the clean face

Open the [Ghòstati! lab](/ghostati/) and let every participant save their face before applying makeup. That enrolment is the local reference against which the system compares later tests. Each participant receives a number and needs to remember it: at the top of the screen, the lab shows the number associated with a face, or whether that face has “escaped” the comparison.

### How do you “win” Ghòstati?

There are two ways. The simplest is for the lab to stop finding a face. It looks similar to what happens when face unlock does not activate or an Instagram filter fails to attach, although here we are observing only the lab’s model.

The second is for the face to be detected, but for the number assigned by the lab to be marked as “escaped”. The comparison uses a tolerance threshold: in our case, when the distance reaches **0.60**, the extracted descriptor is different enough from the saved reference. Being associated with an ID other than your own also means your reference is no longer the nearest one. These results apply to this model and to the conditions of the test.

### Where can I find my number?

{{< media_text src="/images/ghostati/workshops/comandi-save-faces-settings.png" side="left" alt="The Save, Faces and Settings controls in the Ghòstati! lab" caption="Save records the reference; Faces opens the local archive." >}}

Click **Save** to save the face. **Faces** opens the local archive of saved faces, where they can also be deleted. A person can save their face more than once, but for the purposes of the game it is better to remove duplicates.

{{< /media_text >}}

{{< media_text src="/images/ghostati/workshops/ws-lab-save-id.jpg" side="right" alt="The lab with a clean face saved as a reference" caption="Before makeup, the reference face has a distance close to zero." >}}

Ask the person to face the camera and wait for a stable reading. If the face is not detected properly, fix the light, distance and framing first. Starting from a fragile enrolment makes everything that follows difficult to interpret.

{{< /media_text >}}

## 4. Pair up and start with lines

The **1** and **6** brushes are good for details and lines; the **10** works for broad areas and bands. Begin with a colour that is clearly lighter or darker than the skin, then try shapes that cross or interrupt the forehead, eyebrows, bridge of the nose and cheeks. You do not have to cover the whole face.

For historical inspiration, we have always said that it is important to cover and cut across areas of the face considered unique. In workshops, however, we have seen lines, mouse whiskers, little flowers and other drawings challenge this model. You have to try, watch the screen and adjust.

{{< media_text src="/images/ghostati/workshops/ghostati-preparazione-verticale.mp4" side="left" alt="A person wearing makeup checks their face projected on a wall" caption="The person being painted watches the projection and adjusts the pattern: the lab is part of the worktable." >}}

The person painting needs to see the screen too. Makeup is not prepared once and left alone: it changes in response to the test. When participants paint one another, each pair can work almost independently.

{{< /media_text >}}

## 5. Check it at the webcam

{{< media_text src="/images/ghostati/workshops/ghostati-universita-rilevamento-parziale.mp4" side="left" alt="The lab tries to detect a painted face and shows compressed, distorted landmarks" caption="From a single angle the lab finds part of the face again, but the landmarks are compressed and distorted." >}}

In this test the lab loses the face almost all the time. From one angle a box reappears, but the estimated points collect in a small, distorted area. That is a good reason to continue checking, not proof that the makeup works against other models.

{{< /media_text >}}

{{< media_text src="/images/ghostati/workshops/ghostati-verifica-loop.mp4" side="right" alt="A face moves in front of the detection system" caption="A check needs movement and several angles: a single frame is not enough." >}}

Move the face slowly: look straight ahead, then slightly left and right, closer and farther away. If the system still recognises the person, add or move a line and try again. Some people reach a result almost immediately; others do not. A failed attempt is informative too.

{{< /media_text >}}

## 6. Would you like to help the research?

Very extensive makeup can be an excellent first step. Then try reducing it: erase a band, thin a line, change a colour. The artistic and experimental aim is to discover which part produces the effect, not simply to maximise the amount of paint.

If you document the process, take at least three photographs: clean face, work in progress and result.

## 7. Wrapping up

Show people where they can wash and keep water available.

The QR code in the Ghòstati! strip at the top of this page always opens the lab; the thumbnails lead to the gallery. This lets anyone passing by continue the test on their own device without copying the address.
