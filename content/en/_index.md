# The Universal Digital Union

The Universal Digital Union is a space for organisation, research and experimentation against exploitation through digital infrastructure. We begin with a simple observation: the people described as _users_ produce data, train systems and generate value.

We want to turn this shared condition into the capacity to act: by building tools, organising encounters, collecting first-hand reports and documenting what we learn.

---

## From launch to praxis

The Union is no longer only a hypothesis. We organise [Ghòstati! workshops](/en/blog/how-to-run-a-ghostati-workshop/), build tools, collect [confidential reports and inside knowledge](/en/ghostati/report/) and publish what we learn.

Some initiatives are becoming established and producing new tools; others need to be corrected, paused or questioned again. We do not consider this a failure, but part of collective work.

## The fiction of the user

### Digital labour does not look like labour

Every search, photograph, movement, comment and moment of attention produces information. These fragments feed machine-learning systems, predictive models, and advertising and financial infrastructure. A user does not simply consume a service: they are part of the chain that produces its value.[^1][^2]

This labour remains invisible because it is dispersed throughout everyday life, without working hours, a contract or wages. Recognising ourselves as producers of informational value is the first step towards imagining new forms of collective organisation and negotiation.[^3]

## Praxis: experimenting with new methods

Two centuries ago, stopping the production line through a strike allowed industrial workers to make their power visible. Under data capitalism, the traditional strike is no longer enough: production is dispersed throughout everyday life, there is no single workplace to stop, and platforms can absorb individual withdrawal.

We therefore need to experiment with new methods, document their effects and revise them collectively. The five forms of praxis are not a finished rulebook, but directions for work.

## Why a universal union

### A shared condition requires organisation

Digital production does not belong to one profession. It involves anyone who publishes, talks, moves with a device, works through a platform or is classified by an automated system. It is a transnational condition because the infrastructure that enables it — and concentrates its value — is itself transnational.[^4]

A universal union cannot be merely defensive. It must build the capacity to negotiate, protect ourselves and transform our conditions: making systems legible, claiming rights over data and the value we produce, and experimenting with infrastructure consistent with people’s autonomy.
